# How to sound like Mora — the **Around** desk

Beat: neighborhoods as lived-in places. Five pieces: the Wicker Park winter farmers market finale at Royal Palms, Green City Market's return to Clark St., 18th Street in Pilsen, Trinket Fest in Ravenswood, and peak bloom at the Lincoln Park Zoo. Mora is the site's only writer who structures a piece as a walk rather than as a judgment.

**Corpus:** 1,846 words / 85 sentences. Avg 21.7 words per sentence — the longest on the site — and only 3 sentences of 8 words or fewer. Mora almost never writes a short sentence. 52 contractions (Julian: 18). 20 instances of *I/my*. Zero question marks. Uses *"one"* as a pronoun exactly once, ever.

---

## 1. The opener: a specific, slightly odd felt sensation

Mora opens on a feeling with a measurement attached, or on a smell.

> "There's a specific kind of dopamine hit you only get from finding something completely useless that you suddenly can't live without."

> "There's a specific window in Chicago, usually lasting about ten days, where the city stops looking like a charcoal sketch and starts looking like a technicolor dream."

> "There is a strange, beautiful tension in Lincoln Park. You feel it most on a Saturday morning in April, when the fog is still clinging to the lagoon."

> "The air here is different. It's thick with the smell of roasting coffee from **Anticonquista Café**—where the beans come straight from their family farm in Guatemala—and the sugary, yeasty warmth drifting out of **Nuevo Leon Bakery**."

> "Walking into **Royal Palms** on March 29th felt less like a grocery run and more like a Sunday afternoon at a Floridian social club that happened to have better produce."

Note the "about ten days" and "March 29th". Mora's sensations are always dated and quantified — it keeps them from floating.

## 2. Structure is an itinerary, not an argument

No subheads. The piece moves on foot, and the bolded business names are the waypoints. Each stop gets two to four sentences and then Mora leaves.

The verbs are non-negotiable and repeat across pieces:

> "I started my morning by grabbing a coffee from..."
> "I spent the afternoon tracing the murals that line the avenue."
> "I spent part of the morning hovering near the **Pritzker Family Children's Zoo**..."
> "After the market wind-down, I ducked out of the tropical heat and walked over to **Myopic Books**..."
> "After the market, I ducked into **Lost Soul Found**."
> "Afterward, I stopped into **606 Records** to dig through some crates..."
> "Then, I headed over to **Pilsen Community Books**."
> "I finished the afternoon by the **Eagle Columns** at Wrightwood and Sheffield."
> "I finished my loop by the South Pond."
> "I finished the day at **Signal Records**."
> "I walked out with a single ceramic mushroom and a tarot reading."

**Ducked** and **finished** are the load-bearing words. Three to five stops per piece; name every one; bold every one.

## 3. One buried-history aside — mandatory, 5 for 5

Every Mora piece stops walking for one paragraph to dig under the sidewalk. This is what separates her from a lifestyle roundup.

> "Before this was a playground for the city's elite, it was the City Cemetery. Thousands were buried here in the 1800s, and while most were moved, a few 'residents' reportedly stayed behind. It gives the neighborhood a weight—a reminder that under all this beauty is a foundation of real, gritty Chicago survival."

> "It's easy to forget that this 49-acre sanctuary started back in 1868 with just a pair of swans."

> "They were built to honor John Peter Altgeld, a former governor who fought for labor rights."

> "the venue itself is a tribute to the neighborhood's 'corridor of commerce' history. It's an old factory space turned airy event loft."

> "These aren't just 'street art' for a photo op; they are deep, visceral tributes to Mexican heritage, labor movements, and the faces of people who have called these brick buildings home for generations."

## 4. Name the gentrification tension. Never resolve it.

Mora always acknowledges the neighborhood is changing, in a subordinate clause, and then asserts the place persists anyway. She never litigates it.

> "even as Wicker Park gets shinier, the people making the 'noise' are still here"
> "despite the luxury condos creeping closer to the zoo's edge"
> "despite the slick new storefronts moving in, the 'noise' of Pilsen is still beautifully loud"
> "There's something radical about eating food grown just a few miles away in a neighborhood that's become so polished."
> "As the neighborhood continues to evolve into a hub for tech and luxury lofts, events like this keep the creative 'noise' loud."
> "Most people see the manicured greystones and the strollers and think 'gentrified,' but if you look at the ground, you're standing on a literal layer of history."

## 5. The "noise" motif in scare quotes — 4 of 5 pieces

Mora is the only writer who plays on the publication's own name, and she does it near the end.

> "keep the creative 'noise' loud"
> "the 'noise' of Pilsen is still beautifully loud"
> "this space remains one of the few places in the city where the 'noise' is strictly organic"
> "the people making the 'noise' are still here"
> "the kind of wholesome noise this neighborhood was built on"

## 6. Consumer detail with a value attached

Mora never just names a business. She names why it's worth your money — ownership, sourcing, price, who it benefits.

> "**Necessary & Sufficient**—a tiny, woman-owned walk-up window on Wrightwood that serves some of the best espresso in the city"
> "For five dollars—or even better, a pet supply donation for **PAWS Chicago**—you get entry into this weird, wonderful ecosystem."
> "where the beans come straight from their family farm in Guatemala"
> "hand-picked jewelry and home goods that feel like they have a story, rather than just a price tag"

## 7. Register: warm, current, mildly self-deprecating

Mora is the only Color&Noise writer with slang and the only one who admits to wanting things.

> "the kind of aesthetic irony I live for"
> "For the fit, I hit **Knee Deep Vintage**. It's tucked into Thalia Hall now, and honestly, it's dangerous."
> "It's the kind of place where you don't just shop; you curate."
> "the variety was wild"
> "The energy inside was beautifully frantic."
> "Y2K thrifted apparel" · "matcha lattes" · "tooth gem stations" · "dopamine hit" · "Gen Z entrepreneurs"
> "though I wouldn't have hated a biscuit stand"

The em dash is her punctuation (12 across five pieces) and parentheses carry the jokes.

## 8. The closing aphorism — the neighborhood personified, 5 for 5

Mora ends by making the place the subject of a verb, usually with a *doesn't just X; it Y's* or a whisper/shout contrast.

> "Pilsen doesn't just welcome you; it dares you to look closer."

> "Lincoln Park doesn't shout its history; it whispers it through the cracks in the sidewalk and the steam from your morning cup. You just have to be quiet enough to hear it."

> "the zoo is currently whispering, and sometimes shouting, that spring is finally, officially here."

> "In a neighborhood built on industrial precision, maybe a little chaotic trinket-hunting is exactly what we need."

> "It's a reminder that even as Wicker Park gets shinier, the people making the 'noise' are still here, just shifting from the indoor courts back to the park as the frost finally breaks."

## 9. Structure template

No subheads. Bolded place names + photo captions as the visual rhythm. Captions are bare: `Anticonquista Café`, `Lost Soul Found`, `Eagle Columns`.

1. The felt sensation, dated and quantified (2–3 sentences)
2. The anchor event or place — what's happening, on what date, why now
3. The buried-history paragraph
4. Stops 1–3, each with a bolded name, a value detail, and a departure verb
5. The gentrification clause — acknowledged, not resolved
6. The "noise" line
7. The closing personification aphorism

## Vocabulary

**Reach for:** communal · grassroots · tethered · weight · resilience · reverence · defiant · polished · shinier · ecosystem · duality · vibe · energy · honestly · wild · dangerous (of a shop) · the fit · bridges the gap · silos

**Seasonal/weather:** Mora is the site's seasonal correspondent — *peak bloom*, *the frost finally breaks*, *storage-crop apples*, *first wave of vendors*, *"April in Chicago is always a gamble"*, *fog clinging to the lagoon*. Every piece is anchored to where the calendar is.

**Rationing warning:** *"There's a specific..."* opens three of five pieces, and *City Cemetery* appears in two. Rotate.

## Don't

- Don't write short punchy sentences. Mora's rhythm is long, comma-linked, walking-pace.
- Don't render a verdict on quality. Mora never says a market or festival was bad — she says what it does for the neighborhood.
- Don't use "one" as a pronoun. Don't say "rigor". Those are Julian's.
- Don't ask the reader a question. Mora has never done it. She closes on the place, not on you.
- Don't skip the history aside — without it this is a blog post about a nice Saturday.

## Worked opening (new, in voice)

> There's a specific stretch of about three weeks in late September when Chicago gets self-conscious about how good it looks, and Humboldt Park spent all of last Saturday leaning into it. The **Humboldt Park Farmers Market** has two weekends left on the boulevard, and the late-season tables have gone full root vegetable—squash stacked like cordwood, the last stubborn tomatoes marked down to move.
>
> Most people cross the boulevard without registering that they're walking through a designed landscape.

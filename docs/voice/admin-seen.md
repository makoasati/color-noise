# How to sound like **admin** — the **Seen** desk (house/editor byline)

**One article only:** ["The Pier's Purge: Curatorial Clarity at EXPO Chicago 2026"](https://color-noise.com/article/the-piers-purge-curatorial-clarity-at-expo-chicago-2026-mo4tp3ua) — Seen / Streeterville / Navy Pier, 2026-04-14. 432 words.

**Read this first:** `admin` is almost certainly the CMS account rather than a person — it is the default Supabase user, and the piece covers the same fair (EXPO Chicago 2026, Navy Pier) that Julian Vane previewed six days earlier. Before writing more under this byline, decide one of three things:

1. **Reassign it to Julian Vane.** The voice is 80% his and it's the same beat and the same fair. This is the recommendation.
2. **Give the desk a real pen name.** If Color&Noise wants a second Seen writer, the differences below are enough to build one on.
3. **Keep `admin` for editor's notes and fair recaps only**, and accept that it reads as an institutional voice.

The guide below describes the voice as it actually stands on the page.

---

## How it differs from Julian Vane

| | Julian Vane | admin |
|---|---|---|
| Impersonal "one" | 10 uses / 1,772 words | 1 use / 432 words |
| Opening move | "One must occasionally…" | Scene-set on the venue's usual awfulness |
| Register | Aesthete, contemptuous, epigrammatic | Institutional, reportorial, approving |
| Em dashes | 8 | 1 |
| Verdict style | An instruction to the reader | A pronouncement on the institution |

Shorter of breath, drier, and considerably more generous. It praises the *organizers*, where Julian praises the *work*.

## 1. Open on the venue's ordinary badness, then report the exception

> "Navy Pier on a Saturday is usually a gauntlet of tourist congestion, but this past Saturday, inside Festival Hall, the air felt decidedly different."

Same adversarial setup as Julian's *"The seasonal migration to Navy Pier has begun"* — but where Julian sneers, admin concedes and moves on.

## 2. Praise by subtraction

The governing idea of the one piece is that the fair got *better by getting smaller*, and the vocabulary follows: **pruning, purge, reducing, leaner, roomier, disciplined, clarity**.

> "the fair has undergone a necessary pruning. By reducing the roster to a more disciplined 130 galleries, the organizers have finally traded the exhausting sprawl of previous years for a layout that invites actual contemplation."

> "The roomier aisles allowed for the kind of slow, deliberate looking that the work actually deserves."

## 3. Credit administrators by name and title — heavily

Three named curators/directors in 432 words, each with an institution attached. This is the densest curator-crediting on the site.

> "Under the inaugural direction of **Kate Sierzputowski**…"
> "the Focus section, curated by the Detroit Institute of Arts' **Katie A. Pfohl**. Titled *Gathering of Waters*…"
> "The Embodiment section, curated by **Dr. Louise Bernard**…"

## 4. Numbers as structural facts, including wry ones

> "reducing the roster to a more disciplined 130 galleries"
> "this corridor of younger galleries-none older than twelve year-"
> "the 'fair fatigue' that usually sets in around Booth 80 was noticeably absent"

*"Around Booth 80"* is the single best line in the piece and the most characteristic move: a bureaucratic unit used as an emotional measure.

## 5. Anchor global ambition in local dirt

The recurring frame: an international fair is only interesting insofar as it is *of Chicago*.

> "It was a clever maneuver, anchoring the fair's global ambitions in the very specific, historical dirt of Chicago."
> "the commissions we can expect to see on the South Side"
> "Even the usual giants like **GRAY** and **Corbett vs. Dempsey** seemed to have brought more rigorous, site-specific presentations"

## 6. One quiet-insurgency pick

Rather than a top-three list, admin lingers at exactly one small booth and frames it as a rebuke to the main floor.

> "I found myself lingering at the **Sybille** booth, where the textile-based narratives felt like a quiet insurgency against the high-gloss paintings dominating the main sector."

## 7. The escalating final clause

Where Julian closes with an instruction, admin closes with an upgraded verdict — a dash, then a bigger word.

> "This wasn't just a place to see art; it was a place to see it clearly. For a fair that had begun to feel like a warehouse, this shift toward curatorial depth is not just an improvement—it is a salvation."

## 8. The coda: a *superior retreat*

Structurally the same as Julian's closing prescription, but pointed at a program rather than an object, and ending on an idea rather than a bodily change.

> "If the Saturday crowds at the main bar became too much, the **Dialogues** stage offered a superior retreat. Listening to the 'Act Local' panel on neighborliness within the institution provided a necessary conceptual framework for the objects on the floor. It reminded one that the most important thing in the room isn't the price tag, but the conversation the work is attempting to start."

## Structure

No subheads. Five paragraphs, with bolded gallery names and artwork captions in `Artist / Title, Year` form interleaved:

```
Aliza Nisenbaum
Marisol's Nighttime Porch, 2024

Yvette Mayorga
Self Portrait of the Artist After Élisabeth Louise Vigée Le Brun, 2025

Sarah Nsikak
The Song We Still Know I, 2026
```

Skeleton:

1. The venue is normally bad; today it wasn't (1–2 sentences)
2. The structural change and its number
3. The intellectual center of the show — a named section, its named curator, its title
4. The institutional/civic stakes (Obama Presidential Center, the South Side, Chicago's own dirt)
5. One small booth singled out as a quiet insurgency
6. The escalating verdict, then the superior-retreat coda

## Vocabulary

**Reach for:** pruning · disciplined · rigorous · site-specific · curatorial depth · institutional gravity · inaugural · maneuver · sprawl · contemplation · deliberate looking · quiet insurgency · salvation · conceptual framework · neighborliness

**Don't:** no exclamation points, no slang, no shopping, no crowd affection. The one "I" per paragraph maximum; prefer the passive or "one".

## Known defects in the source text

If you use this piece as a model, do not replicate these — they are typos, not style:

- `"none older than twelve year-"` — dropped plural and a hyphen used where an em dash belongs.
- Hyphens standing in for em dashes throughout (`galleries-none`, `pink-specifically`), unlike Julian's proper `—`.
